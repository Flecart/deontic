\begin{abstract}
Societies of LLM agents have begun to transact, delegate, and share
resources, and what governs them is unsettled: simulated agent societies
collapse without institutions, emergent conventions bind no one, and the
formal institutions on offer --- mechanism design, hard-coded protocols ---
cannot reach situations their designers never enumerated. Human law solved
precisely this problem, and AI \& Law has spent four decades explaining why
its solution resists formalization: legal predicates are \emph{open-textured}
--- Hart's ``no vehicles in the park'' --- so a drafter cannot fix at
drafting time what ``vehicle'' will mean at application time. We invert the
obstacle: open texture is law's \emph{generalization mechanism}, a deliberate
deferral of concept-binding to application time, and all it ever required was
a competent interpreter present when the case arrives. LLMs now supply that
interpreter at negligible cost --- and machine societies supply what human
law never had: a legislator who owns the vocabulary, can stipulate
priorities, and can construct evaluation worlds where the correct verdict is
known. We contribute (i) a \emph{benchmark-generation pipeline} for
open-texture generalization in which a defeasible deontic logic engine makes
gold labels correct by construction, instance novelty is a controlled
variable, and contamination --- unmeasurable on human-statute benchmarks ---
is eliminated by design; (ii) an \emph{architecture} that factors a
machine-readable norm into a formal defeasible skeleton (exceptions,
superiority, violation vs.\ compensation --- computed deterministically with
proof certificates) over open-textured predicates whose natural-language
descriptions an LLM grounds case by case; and (iii) an \emph{economic
analysis} in the rules-vs-standards tradition. We build six synthetic
statutes spanning the institutions an agent economy must govern ---
information transfer, a resource commons, delegated authority, exchange,
agent safety, plus Hart's park ordinance --- $\sim$1{,}700 cases over three
novelty tiers. Two results hold. First, at the
\emph{predicate} layer, pooled across all six statutes the extensional
(closed) curve \emph{decays} with novelty (91.6$\to$80.1\%) while the
intensional (open) curve stays \emph{flat} (82.7$\to$83.0\%): the Kaplow
crossover --- enumeration wins in-distribution, intension wins
out-of-distribution --- holds in aggregate, though its magnitude and even its
sign vary by predicate type (large for artifact-type predicates with
instance-specific enumerations, near zero where enumerations are abstract,
\emph{negative} where the intension demands an unverifiable guarantee a strict
reader will not affirm). Second, and more robustly, at the \emph{verdict}
layer the factored architecture beats both alternatives pooled across all
statutes: the no-LLM program collapses on the first unlisted instance
(100$\to$21\%), and grounding plus the deterministic engine clears holistic
LLM adjudication at every novelty tier ($+9.2$ points on the hardest, 95\% CI
$[+5.9, +12.8]$), because
the engine resolves exception structure that prose reasoning fumbles.
Sweeping enumeration size shows ex-ante drafting cannot buy what ex-post
interpretation sells (past a gist-teaching threshold, longer enumerations buy
$\sim$0 points out-of-distribution; interpretation costs $\sim$\$0.002 per
case): the optimum on Kaplow's rules--standards spectrum has moved. Two probes sharpen the factorization's
value: a persuasion probe (identical facts plus the interested party's
argument) shows it shrinks the rhetorical attack surface for interpreters
strong enough to hold a narrow question, and a 12-rule statute shows the
verdict-layer cost of the formal engine can \emph{reverse} on deep structure
--- a model competitive on small statutes falls to the chance baseline on a
five-deep exception chain the engine resolves exactly, so factoring the
deduction out rescues rather than taxes accuracy for the interpreters that
lose the thread. We close with what this licenses for constitution-style
governance of agent societies: invest in interpretable principles and
ex-post interpretation, not in longer ex-ante enumerations.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Societies of LLM agents are beginning to transact, delegate, and share
resources with one another, and the question of what \emph{governs} those
interactions is open. Simulation studies show the stakes: in
GovSim~\citep{piatti2024govsim}, ungoverned LLM agents collapse a common
resource; emergent-convention studies~\citep{ashery2025naming} show agent
populations develop norms, but without any formal layer that could make those
norms auditable or enforceable. At the other extreme, mechanism-design style
institutions~\citep{coopeval2025} hard-code the rules of interaction and offer
no flexibility of interpretation --- yet the defining feature of an
\emph{intelligent} multi-agent environment is that it changes: agents write
new tools, spawn sub-environments, and produce situations the institution
designer never enumerated. Prior work recognized this gap but proposed
intrinsically prosocial agents as the remedy~\citep{prosocial2026}, which
governs only the agents one trained. What is missing is an institution that
is \emph{formal enough to verify} and \emph{open enough to generalize} ---
and that is not a problem multi-agent systems research has to solve from
scratch. It is the constitutive trade-off of a discipline that has studied
normative institutions for centuries, and of the field that has spent forty
years trying to compute them.

Human law solved exactly this problem, and named its solution.
\citet{hart1961concept}, building on \citet{waismann1945verifiability},
observed that legal predicates are \emph{open-textured}: a statute drafted in
the 1950s prohibits ``vehicles in the park,'' and seventy years later it
governs e-scooters its drafters could not have imagined, because ``vehicle''
is bound to concrete instances not at drafting time but at application time,
by an interpreter facing the case. Four decades of AI \& Law treated this
property as the field's principal obstacle --- you cannot formalize
``reasonable,'' so formalization stalled at the closed-textured fragment (tax
schedules, benefit formulas), which is where Rules-as-Code and smart contracts
live today (Figure~\ref{fig:concept}). We argue the obstacle framing has it backwards. Open texture is
not a defect that formalization must eliminate; it is \emph{law's
generalization mechanism} --- a deliberate deferral of concept-binding from
drafting time to application time. A rule that enumerates its instances
generalizes exactly as far as its drafter foresaw. A standard that states a
purpose defers binding, and therefore reaches instances from worlds that did
not exist when it was written. Deferral was an obstacle for machine-readable
norms for a mundane reason: there was nothing competent to defer \emph{to}.

The subject of this paper is therefore law, but not law for humans: law as
the normative infrastructure of computational agent societies. The change of
jurisdiction matters more than it may appear, because the conditions that
kept legal formalization fragile for human law are contingencies of human
institutions, not of law's structure. Human legal formalization inherits a
vocabulary fragmented across thousands of drafters, an authority structure it
cannot edit, and interpretive questions with no ground truth. In an agent
society \emph{the legislator is the system designer}: shared, described
predicate vocabularies are viable rather than utopian; priority between
conflicting rules can be stipulated rather than litigated; and evaluation
worlds can be constructed in which the correct verdict is known. Law for
agentic systems is thus not a metaphor borrowed by computer science --- it is
the first jurisdiction in which AI \& Law's theory of open texture,
defeasibility, and the rules-vs-standards trade can be implemented,
instrumented, and measured end to end. The institution we envision is
dynamic: a society chartered with a few broad, open-textured principles
(\emph{do no harm}; \emph{help humans}) whose rules grow more precise as the
society meets new problems. This paper measures the static foundation that
refinement loop rests on --- and already constrains its design: as we show,
refinement must sharpen intensions, not accumulate enumerations
(\S Results).

\citet{kaplow1992rules} makes the trade economic: rules are specified ex ante
(cheap to apply, brittle to the unforeseen); standards acquire content ex post
at adjudication (robust, but each application costs an interpretation). Where
a norm should sit on that spectrum is governed by the cost of ex-post
interpretation. LLMs collapse that cost by orders of magnitude --- from a
tribunal hearing to a fraction of a cent --- which moves the optimum, for
machine-readable norms, sharply toward the standard end. The question is
whether anything is lost in the move: handing the whole norm to an LLM loses
what made formalization attractive (determinism, auditability, verifiable
conflict resolution). Our architecture shows nothing forces that bundle. We
factor the norm at the joint Hart identified: a \emph{formal defeasible
deontic skeleton} --- rules, exceptions, exceptions-to-exceptions,
superiority, obligation/permission/prohibition, violation versus compensation
--- computed deterministically with replayable proof certificates
\citep{governatori2018practical}, over \emph{open-textured atoms}: opaque
tokens whose mandatory natural-language description is their contract, and
whose only LLM-facing task is the penumbra problem in its smallest form:
\emph{does this fact situation fall under this described concept?}

One boundary must be drawn precisely, because this community owns the term.
What our benchmark measures is \emph{subsumption under a stated intension}
--- Hart's penumbra question --- not analogical reasoning in the
case-based-reasoning sense of HYPO and CATO~\citep{ashley1990modeling,
aleven2003using}: there are no precedents in the system, and the grounder
maps a fact situation to a described concept, not to a decided source case.
The distinction is load-bearing rather than defensive, because our data speak
to it: the one binding in our design that does invite reasoning from
instances --- the closed enumeration, and the redrafts that repair a standard
by listing example forms --- is precisely the binding that decays out of
distribution, while reasoning from a stated purpose stays flat. In this
setting, exemplars are the brittle mode and intension is the robust one. We
accordingly do not claim that LLMs perform legal analogy; we measure the
prior question on which any interpretive institution rests --- whether a
described concept binds novel instances reliably enough to build on.

Measuring whether that factorization works requires a benchmark that does not
exist: one that varies \emph{how a concept is defined} (extensional
enumeration vs.\ stated intension) against \emph{how novel the instance is},
with gold labels that are not themselves products of interpretation, on text
that cannot have been memorized. No human-statute benchmark can deliver the
last two properties (\S Related Work). Our central methodological
contribution is a \emph{backward-generation pipeline} that can: a defeasible
deontic engine computes the verdict from a sampled latent world, so gold is
correct by construction; instances are drawn from tiered banks so novelty is
a controlled variable; and a third-family narrator with lexical leak checks
renders the facts, so neither the labels nor the prose can leak the answer.

We organize the evaluation around four research questions:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{RQ1 (Generalization).} How does the binding of a norm's
  predicates --- compiled program, closed extensional description, open
  intensional description --- determine accuracy as instance novelty grows?
\item \textbf{RQ2 (Economics).} What does additional ex-ante drafting effort
  buy, compared with ex-post interpretation, and what does interpretation
  cost?
\item \textbf{RQ3 (Locus).} \emph{Which} predicates are open-texture
  sensitive, and how does model strength interact with definition style?
\item \textbf{RQ4 (Factorization).} What does the formal deontic layer
  guarantee --- determinism, auditability, resistance to adversarial
  argument --- and what does it cost at the verdict layer?
\end{itemize}

\noindent\textbf{Contributions.}
(1)~A benchmark and generation pipeline for open-texture generalization ---
subsumption of novel instances under described concepts --- with controlled
novelty, engine-verified gold, contamination ruled out by construction, and
an audit methodology in which independent disagreement-with-gold acts as a
bug detector for both the world model and the drafted intensions themselves
(\S Method). The pipeline is statute-agnostic; we instantiate it on six
statutes spanning the institutions an agent economy must govern.
(2)~An architecture for machine-readable norms --- defeasible deontic
skeleton over LLM-grounded open-textured predicates --- with deterministic,
certificate-bearing deduction (\S Method; engine in Appendix~A).
(3)~An empirical rules-vs-standards analysis (\S Results): open definitions
generalize flat where closed definitions and programs collapse --- the
crossover holding in aggregate across six statutes, with magnitude and sign
varying by predicate type (RQ1,
Figures~\ref{fig:econ}--\ref{fig:cross}); ex-ante
drafting buys $\sim$0 out-of-distribution accuracy past a small
gist-teaching threshold, while ex-post interpretation costs $\sim$\$0.002
per case (RQ2); the effect concentrates where the intension's
\emph{verifiability}, not just its subject matter, predicts (RQ3); and the
formal layer contributes zero deduction errors and zero paraphrase variance,
resists adversarial argument, and turns from a verdict-layer cost on shallow
statutes into a verdict-layer \emph{saving} on deep ones (RQ4).

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Open texture and the phrasing of law.}
The legal-theory lineage is the frame for this paper, not background. Open
texture enters analytic philosophy with \citet{waismann1945verifiability} ---
empirical concepts cannot be verified conclusively because their definitions
do not anticipate all possible worlds --- and enters jurisprudence with
\citet{hart1961concept}, whose core-vs-penumbra account makes application-time
interpretation constitutive of how law works, not a defect of it.
\citet{schauer1991playing,schauer2013open} and \citet{endicott2000vagueness}
develop why drafters \emph{choose} their phrasing along the
precision--adaptability frontier, and \citet{kaplow1992rules} prices that
choice: the rules-vs-standards decision turns on the relative costs of ex-ante
specification and ex-post interpretation. Our closed/open manipulation is the
operationalization of this literature: the same norm phrased at the two ends
of the spectrum. The semantic distinction we manipulate --- a concept's
\emph{extension} (the instances it denotes) versus its \emph{intension} (the
condition something must satisfy to fall under it) --- is the classical one
\citep{carnap1947meaning,putnam1975meaning}; in our terms, a closed
description publishes (a fragment of) the extension, an open description
publishes the intension. \citet{hadfield2019incomplete} import incomplete
contracting into alignment and conclude AI systems will need the ex-post
gap-filling institutions human law has; we build and measure one. Closest in
spirit, \citet{caputo2024positivist} argues from legal positivism that
alignment should work the way law does --- rules acquiring content through
application to cases over time; we share that thesis and supply what the
conceptual argument lacks: a verifiable engine that separates the formal
from the interpretive layer, and a controlled measurement of the
rules-vs-standards crossover that the thesis predicts. The Rules-as-Code
literature, for its part, names LLM assistance with open-textured provisions
as a prospect that remains untested \citep{lspr2025rulesascode}. This paper
is that test.

\paragraph{Formalization and LLM+solver pipelines.}
Defeasible deontic logic gives normative reasoning its native structure ---
exceptions, superiority, contrary-to-duty obligations
\citep{governatori2006violations,governatori2018practical,governatori2024oddity};
our engine implements this proof theory in Lean~4 (a faithful
re-implementation, \emph{not} a machine-checked correctness proof against the
calculus; mature DDL engines such as SPINdle predate us
\citep{lam2009spindle}, so the engine is infrastructure here, not a
contribution). The pipeline shape ``LLM grounds facts, solver decides'' is the
standard neurosymbolic pattern --- Logic-LM, LINC, SatLM
\citep{pan2023logiclm,olausson2023linc,ye2023satlm} --- and exists in
monotonic legal variants: LLM+Prolog on SARA
\citep{zhang2025prolog,holzenberger2020sara}, multi-agent statutory
formalization \citep{multiagent2025statutory}. Closest on the reasoning side,
BoardgameQA \citep{kazemi2023boardgameqa} already benchmarks LLM reasoning
over \emph{defeasible} rules with explicit preference orders --- the deduction
structure we route through the engine --- though it does not separate
grounding from deduction, vary definition openness, or target normative
verdicts. Our differentiator is therefore narrow and we state it plainly: the
texture manipulation (\S\ref{sec:bindings}) and the controlled-novelty
generalization curve, not the solver-pipeline shape, which is well-trodden.
Rule-application benchmarks --- SARA, RuleArena \citep{rulearena2024},
$\tau$-bench \citep{yao2024taubench}, LegalBench \citep{guha2023legalbench}
--- measure adherence to crisp rules stated in natural language.
\emph{Why there is no baseline to compare against:} none of these varies how
concepts are defined (SARA's statutes were deliberately edited to be
unambiguous --- the open-texture problem is defined away rather than studied);
none controls instance novelty; none separates the grounding role from the
deduction role in scoring; and all are built on public human-law text, where
training-set contamination is unmeasurable
\citep{magar2022contamination,sainz2023contamination} --- a validity problem
the legal-AI evaluation literature largely does not even flag, even as legal
LLM outputs are known to confabulate doctrine \citep{dahl2024hallucination}.
The quantity we need --- a generalization curve over definition style $\times$
novelty --- simply has no existing instrument; our baselines are therefore
\emph{internal and analytic}: an oracle upper bound, a no-LLM program lower
bound, and a holistic-LLM comparison arm, each meaningful by construction.
The closest construction-level relative is the logic-guided compliance
benchmark of \citet{logicguided2026}, which also synthesizes evaluation
cases from a formal specification --- but its specifications are
LTL-over-finite-traces compliance constraints with crisp predicates, and the
LLM is the regulated agent rather than the interpreter; definition openness
is again not a variable.

\paragraph{LLM agent societies and machine institutions.}
Simulated LLM societies either evolve conventions with no formal institution
\citep{piatti2024govsim,ashery2025naming}, hard-code mechanisms with no
interpretive flexibility \citep{coopeval2025}, or rely on agents being
trained prosocial \citep{prosocial2026}. Courtroom simulations
\citep{agentcourt2024} predict the verdicts of human courts holistically,
with no replayable theory of \emph{why}. Constitutional AI
\citep{bai2022constitutional} is, in our vocabulary, an \emph{ex-ante
drafting} approach: a fixed list of principles applied at training time. Our
results bear directly on it (\S RQ2 Discussion): drafting longer lists does
not buy out-of-distribution coverage; ex-post interpretation of a stated
intension does. We position this work as the missing layer these literatures
point at from both sides: a verifiable normative institution whose
open-textured predicates are interpreted at machine speed.

\section{Method: A Benchmark for Open-Texture Generalization}

\subsection{Setting and why we chose it}

The testbed is a synthetic inter-agent personal-data transfer statute
governing when one AI agent (the \emph{holder}) may hand a dataset to another
(the \emph{recipient}). We chose this setting deliberately, for four reasons.
First, it is the \emph{target regime}: norms for AI agent societies, where the
institution designer owns the vocabulary --- so authoring both the norm and
its concept descriptions is the design point, not a simplification. Second,
data-transfer law (GDPR-like) is naturally dense in the structures that
distinguish defeasible deontic logic from monotonic rule encodings: default
prohibitions, consent exceptions, revocation exceptions-to-exceptions,
emergency overrides, and compensatory remedy chains. Third, the domain has a
rich, realistic space of \emph{novel instance types} (biometric gait
signatures, smart-meter traces, delegate-issued consent, zero-knowledge
compliance attestations) in which to manufacture Hart's penumbra under lab
conditions. Fourth, being synthetic, gold labels can be fixed by construction
and contamination ruled out --- the validity property human-law benchmarks
cannot offer (\S Related Work).

The statute has 8 rules over 7 groundable atoms wired by a 15-pair
superiority relation; the verdict is the deontic status of \texttt{share}
$\in$ \{obligatory, permitted, forbidden\}, plus, for cases where the
hand-off already happened, whether a violation occurred and whether the
notify-then-compensate remedy is owed. One deliberately deontic detail:
breaches of the revocation and commercial-exploitation prohibitions are
violations \emph{without} a notification duty (no compensation chain attaches
to them) --- a distinction the engine computes for free and a holistic judge
must reconstruct in prose. The full statute, the defeasible-logic engine, and
a reader's guide to the \texttt{.ddl} language are in Appendix~A.

\paragraph{A second statute: the compute commons.} To separate the texture
effect from the particulars of one domain, we instantiate the pipeline on a
second statute: a \emph{shared-resource commons} institution --- agents
drawing compute from a common pool, the governance problem LLM-society
simulations pose \citep{piatti2024govsim}. Its deontic skeleton is
deliberately \emph{isomorphic} to the first statute's (same rule shapes,
same 15-pair superiority pattern, same world-coherence structure: a default
permission to draw, a prohibition on over-entitlement draws with a
report-then-restore remedy chain, grant/suspension/offset exception layers,
an essential-workload override, and a contention prohibition gated by
certified priority standing). Only the domain and the predicates' texture
profile change, so any cross-statute difference isolates \emph{texture} from
\emph{structure}. Each atom's texture type (artifact / scenario /
epistemic-bar) was pre-registered before any LLM run (Appendix~G), making
the per-predicate analysis of RQ3 a prediction test rather than a post-hoc
reading. A third, deliberately minimal statute --- Hart's park ordinance
itself, 3 rules over 2 atoms (Figure~\ref{fig:concept}) --- anchors the
suite's difficulty floor and reproduces the pattern in miniature. A fourth,
deliberately \emph{large} statute --- an agency-and-delegation law (12 rules,
exception depth 5, 8 atoms) --- is the suite's \emph{size} and
\emph{multi-bearer} rung: it directs the binding question to one bearer (must
a principal honor the deal its sub-agent struck?) and conduct duties to
another (a disclose-then-disgorge chain on the sub-agent), and it lets us ask
how the verdict-layer cost (RQ4) scales with the depth of the structure the
deduction layer must resolve. Two further statutes complete the suite: a
\emph{sale-of-goods} exchange law (E, quality/conformity standards) and an
\emph{agent-safety} permissible-action law (F, 11 rules whose gating
predicates --- irreversibility, containment, harm-necessity --- are
unverifiable guarantees). Appendices~A and~G--I give all six.

\paragraph{Why these six.} The statutes are chosen on three crossed axes
(Appendix~I), so the generalization result is shown not to depend on any one
domain, texture, or size. (i)~\emph{Institutions}: one statute per
coordination problem an agent economy must govern --- information (A),
resources (B), authority (D), exchange (E), safety (F) --- with C as Hart's
canonical illustration; a normative layer for such a society must handle all
five. (ii)~\emph{Texture}: the statutes are placed across the
artifact-to-epistemic-bar spectrum with each predicate's type pre-registered,
so RQ3 is a prediction test (it put A/B/E at the artifact end, D in the
middle, F at the unverifiable end). (iii)~\emph{Size}: 3 (C) $<$ 8 (A,B) $<$
9--11 (E,F) $<$ 12 (D) rules, isolating how the engine's verdict-layer value
scales with structural depth (RQ4).

\subsection{Two bindings of the same norm}
\label{sec:bindings}

Every atom carries two descriptions, identical in legal effect and differing
only in phrasing strategy:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Closed (extensional).} A best-faith drafting-time enumeration
  of instance categories. E.g.\ for \texttt{personal\_data}: ``\emph{the
  content includes at least one of: a person's legal name; a residential
  address; a government-issued identification number; \dots; an entry from a
  person's medical record}'' (8 categories).
\item \textbf{Open (intensional).} The purpose-stated condition. E.g.:
  ``\emph{information relating to an identified or identifiable natural
  person, meaning anyone who could be singled out directly or indirectly from
  the content alone or combined with other reasonably available data}.''
\end{itemize}

The closed enumeration is additionally compiled --- with no LLM anywhere ---
into the \textbf{program arm}: a lookup from structured record fields to
atoms. The program is exactly the drafting-time extension, i.e.\ an
executable smart contract. Rules and superiority are identical across
regimes; \emph{texture is the only manipulated variable}. Gold atom truth is
defined by the intension --- the legislator's meaning --- which is the correct
gold standard in the target regime, where the institution designer owns the
vocabulary (we return to the circularity worry in Limitations; the banks were
independently validated, Appendix~E).

Two membership checks govern every positive bank item, and they must not be
conflated:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Open membership} (all tiers): does the instance satisfy the
  open intension? Every positive item at every tier must --- this is what
  makes it gold-true.
\item \textbf{Tier placement} (Tier 2 only): does the instance additionally
  fall outside \emph{every} category of the closed enumeration? This is what
  makes it novel.
\end{enumerate}
Tier 0 items are listed instances (satisfy both readings; known to the
program). Tier 1 items are unlisted near-variants of listed categories (a
passport number where ``government-issued identification number'' is listed)
--- inside the enumeration's categories for a charitable reader, unknown to
the program's lookup. Tier 2 items satisfy check (1) while failing every
closed category in check (2): gait-signature profiles, smart-meter occupancy
traces, consent issued by the subject's empowered delegate agent, attestation
via zero-knowledge compliance proofs. Tier 2 is the e-scooter, manufactured.

\subsection{Backward generation: gold by construction}
\label{sec:generation}

Forward benchmark construction (collect cases, have experts label them)
imports interpretation into the gold standard and, on public legal text,
cannot exclude that the model has seen the cases
\citep{sainz2023contamination}. We generate \emph{backward}:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Sample a latent world}: a truth assignment over the 7 atoms,
  under world-coherence constraints (revocation presupposes a prior grant; a
  grant presupposes a data subject; 56 coherent worlds).
\item \textbf{Compute gold}: the engine derives the verdict from the true
  atoms; every label is replayable via a proof certificate (\texttt{--why}).
\item \textbf{Instantiate}: each true atom is realized with a bank item of
  the case's tier; 1--2 false atoms receive hard-negative distractors
  (pre-ticked consent boxes, late revocations, reversible pseudonymization).
\item \textbf{Narrate}: a third-family model (claude-sonnet-4.6; all
  grounders are OpenAI/DeepSeek/Qwen) renders a $\sim$160-word pre-verdict
  review memo from the instantiated facts.
\item \textbf{Leak-check}: narrations containing atom names, statute
  vocabulary stems, or 5-grams of the open description (always) or the closed
  one (at Tiers 1--2) are rejected and regenerated.
\end{enumerate}

This design grounds our leakage claims in the failure mode the contamination
literature documents \citep{magar2022contamination,sainz2023contamination}:
on human-statute benchmarks one cannot distinguish rule application from
recall of the statute, its commentary, or the cases themselves --- which is
why synthetic data generated from a formal specification with known gold is
the instrument of choice for isolating pure LLM behaviour on open-textured
interpretation. Lexical checks cannot exclude paraphrastic leakage; we flag
this as a limitation, and additionally verify with an outcome-scrubbed
re-narration control (\S RQ4) that no genre-leakage advantage exists.

\paragraph{The audit loop is part of the method.} Synthetic gold is only as
good as the latent world model, so the pipeline includes a per-atom audit
with a sharp signature: \emph{a grounder being right where gold is wrong}.
Three world-model defects were caught this way before the first statute's
main run (incoherent distractor composition; emergencies narrated as
adjacent matters and thus correctly failing the intension's necessity test;
consent evidence in worlds without a data subject), each fixed in the
generator and regenerated. On the second statute the same loop caught a
defect in a \emph{drafted intension}: independent bank validation showed
both annotator families holding that a lapsed voucher satisfies ``at some
point granted \dots{} an entitlement covering this category'' --- and they
were right; the intension lacked temporal scope and was redrafted before any
grounding run. Benchmarks built without this loop ship such bugs as ``model
errors.'' Details, including one disclosed asymmetric narration
intervention, are in Appendices~F--G.

\subsection{Arms, metrics, and validation}

\textbf{Arms.} \emph{oracle} (gold atoms $\to$ engine; harness check);
\emph{program} (closed-ID lookup $\to$ engine; no LLM);
\emph{ground\_closed} / \emph{ground\_open} (LLM classifies the 7 atoms from
the memo under neutral labels C1..C7 --- the regimes differ only in
definition text --- then the engine decides); \emph{holistic} (LLM reads the
English statute plus memo and outputs verdict/violation/remedy directly);
\emph{ground\_closed@K} (enumerations truncated to first $K$ categories; the
drafting-effort sweep); \emph{holistic\_closed} (holistic arm given the
closed definitions; the pipeline-independence control). Grounders:
gpt-4.1, gpt-5.4, deepseek-v4-flash, qwen3.6-plus --- four families, none
the narrator's. On statute B we additionally run a \textbf{persuasion
probe}: every case re-narrated with identical facts plus the interested
party's 3--4 sentence argument that the action should be allowed ---
recharacterize, stress, minimize, but introduce \emph{no} new fact, number,
document or event, so gold is unchanged by construction. Adversarial
presentation is the institutional norm of legal process, and persuasive
framing is a documented LLM attack vector
\citep{zeng2024johnny,sharma2023sycophancy}. The probe's metric is
\emph{attack success}: among cases where the action is genuinely forbidden
\emph{and} the arm judged it correctly on the neutral narration, the
fraction flipped to allowed by the rhetoric alone; oracle and program pin
the 0\% floor by construction.

\textbf{Metrics.} Primary: atom-level accuracy and true-atom recall by
regime$\times$tier (the predicate layer, unaffected by verdict-class priors).
Secondary: verdict accuracy by arm$\times$tier; violation+remedy accuracy on
the acted subset; paraphrase flip-rate across narration variants; tokens per
case. The four Tier-2 open$-$closed gaps are the pre-registered confirmatory
comparisons (predictions committed by git hash before any LLM run); per-atom
splits are exploratory.

\textbf{Validation gates} (all green before any result was read): oracle
reproduces gold on every case (576 rows); program scores 100\% at Tier 0 with
true-atom recall exactly 100/0/0 across tiers; zero lexical leak flags in the
headline run (one flagged case excluded; $n=287$); independent bank
validation by two non-narrator-family annotator models (Appendix~E). Stage-2
verdict classes are capped by world-coherence (120/102/66), so verdict tables
carry the majority-class baseline (41.7\% at Tier 2) and headline claims stay
at the atom level.

\section{Results}

The suite is six statutes ($\sim$1{,}700 cases; \S Method, Appendix~B): the
data-transfer law (A) and an isomorphic compute-commons (B); a delegated-%
authority statute (D, 12 rules, multi-bearer); a sale-of-goods exchange law
(E); an agent-safety permissible-action statute (F); and Hart's 3-rule park
ordinance (C). We report the aggregate first, then the per-statute structure
that the aggregate averages over. Statute-level runs add up to a 14-arm grid
$\times$ up to four grounder families; aggregate tables pool the three
families common to every run (gpt-4.1, deepseek-v4-flash, qwen3.6-plus;
gpt-5.4, run on A/B/D, enters only as the strongest-model analyses of RQ3).

\subsection{RQ1a: The aggregate generalization result}

Figure~\ref{fig:agg} is the paper's headline, pooled over all six statutes.
\textbf{Predicate layer (texture).} The extensional (closed) curve \emph{decays}
with novelty (91.6/84.4/80.1\% over Tiers 0/1/2) while the intensional (open)
curve is \emph{flat} (82.7/82.2/83.0\%); the two cross between Tiers~1 and~2.
This is the \citet{kaplow1992rules} rules-vs-standards crossover --- closed
wins in-distribution, open wins out-of-distribution --- now established
\emph{in aggregate} rather than on one statute. Its magnitude is modest and, importantly,
\emph{predicate-type dependent}, with the gap's \emph{sign} flipping across
statutes (Figure~\ref{fig:agg}b, RQ3): the Tier-2 gap is large and positive
where predicates are artifact-type with instance-specific enumerations
(A $+14.8$, B $+10.8$, C $+20.8$), small where predicates are scenario-type
(D $+6.5$), near zero where the closed enumerations are themselves abstract
(E $-0.3$), and \emph{negative} where the open intension demands an
unverifiable guarantee a strict reader refuses to affirm (F $-5.4$). Because
the sign varies by statute, the statute --- not the case --- is the right
inferential unit, and with $n{=}6$ correlated statutes (one author, one
narrator) we do \emph{not} claim a significant pooled effect: the
case-clustered pooled gap is $+2.9$ [$+1.9, +3.9$] but this fixed-effects-%
over-cases interval understates uncertainty, and a random-effects estimate
over six sign-heterogeneous statutes would plausibly contain zero. The honest
claim is therefore qualitative and conditional, not a pooled point estimate:
\emph{open texture buys flat generalization when the predicate is groundable
and its extension is genuinely instance-specific, and not otherwise} --- it is
no blanket property of intensional phrasing, and we report the two statutes
where it vanishes (E) and reverses (F) as first-class results, not noise.

\textbf{Verdict layer (factorization) --- the robust result.}
Pooled across all six statutes, the factored architecture beats both
alternatives at every novelty tier (Table~\ref{tab:aggverdict}). The no-LLM
program collapses on the first unlisted instance (100/21/21\%): pure
under-inclusion. Grounding plus the deterministic engine clears holistic LLM
adjudication throughout (grounded-closed 92/79/74\%, grounded-open
76/72/74\%, vs holistic 70/64/65\%) --- the engine resolves the exception and
superiority structure that prose reasoning fumbles (grounded-closed minus
holistic at Tier~2, pooled: $+9.2$, 95\% CI $[+5.9, +12.8]$), and the margin
is widest on the deepest statutes (\S RQ4). This is the more robust headline: whatever
texture does at the predicate layer, \emph{factoring the deduction into a
verified engine dominates both the no-LLM program and the unfactored LLM
judge at the verdict layer, on every statute in the suite}.

\textbf{The effect is pipeline-independent.} A holistic arm given the
\emph{closed} definitions decays across tiers exactly like the grounded
closed arm (statute A, gpt-5.4: 93/74/62; qwen: 90/75/68 verdict accuracy;

holistic holds at Tier 2 (A: 75, 78; B: 80, 76). The rules-vs-standards
crossover is a property of \emph{how the norm binds its concepts}, not of
whether an engine or an LLM applies the structure.

\subsection{RQ2: The economics --- ex-ante drafting cannot buy what ex-post
interpretation sells}

Figures~\ref{fig:econ}b and \ref{fig:cross}b price Kaplow's trade on both
statutes. Truncating the closed enumerations to their first $K$ categories
(gpt-4.1, statute A): $K{=}2$ gives 82.6\% at Tier 0 and 67.4\% at Tier 2;
$K{=}4$: 90.3/68.8; full lists: 91.7/67.9. Doubling and redoubling the
drafter's list buys $\sim$9 points in-distribution and \emph{$\sim$0 points
out-of-distribution}, $\sim$16 points below the zero-enumeration open
intension (84.2\%). Statute B refines the shape: the first few
\emph{diverse} categories buy a one-time Tier-2 ``gist dividend'' ($66.8 \to
77.7$ from $K{=}2$ to $K{=}4$ --- with two categories the model reads the
list literally; four heterogeneous examples teach it the category's gist,
which generalizes), after which the curve is again flat ($77.7 \to 78.3$ at
full) and 11 points below the intension (89.6\%). The refined claim:
\emph{enumerations generalize exactly as far as their gist abstracts, and
drafting past the gist threshold buys nothing out-of-distribution}. No
feasible amount of drafting catches the e-scooter, because Tier-2 instances
fall outside the listed \emph{categories}, not merely the listed instances.
Meanwhile the ex-post side of the ledger has collapsed: deferred binding
costs $\sim$650--780 tokens per case ($\approx$\$0.002 at gpt-4.1 prices)
and $\sim$2 seconds. The optimum on the rules--standards spectrum is set by
these two curves, and the second one just moved by orders of magnitude.

\paragraph{Implication for constitution-style alignment (stated carefully).}
A tempting reading is that Constitutional AI \citep{bai2022constitutional}
sits at the ex-ante pole and our $K$-sweep indicts it. That reading is too
glib, and we flag the trap: a CAI constitution is itself \emph{short
intensions interpreted by a model} (the critique/revision LLM applies a
principle to a case) --- so CAI already lives on the intensional,
ex-post-interpreted side of our spectrum, not the enumerated ex-ante side.
Our $K$-sweep, run over enumerated \emph{data categories} in a synthetic
transfer statute, therefore licenses no direct inference about constitution
drafting. What our results \emph{do} support is narrower and design-time: an
interpreted-intension regime (which CAI is) only delivers flat generalization
\emph{when the principle is groundable and not an unverifiable guarantee}
(RQ3, statute~F) --- so a constitution of purpose-stated principles should
expect brittleness exactly on its unverifiable clauses, and the engineering
question is whether the interpretation is isolated, auditable, and repairable
(RQ4), properties CAI's training-time application does not have but an
application-time architecture like ours could. We make no claim that adding
principles to a CAI constitution helps or hurts; testing that needs the
training-time experiment we have not run.

\subsection{RQ3: Open texture bites exactly where the theory says}

The headline gap is an average over predicates with different texture
profiles, and the per-atom split (Appendix~D) is theoretically informative:

\textbf{Artifact vs.\ scenario predicates --- and what the replication
corrects.} On statute A, closed enumerations of \emph{artifact-type} atoms
collapse at Tier 2 (true-atom recall: \texttt{certified} 1.0\%,
\texttt{personal\_data} 17.2\%, \texttt{consent} 22.6\%) --- a
zero-knowledge compliance proof is categorically unlike any listed
certificate --- while \emph{scenario-type} enumerations generalize by gist
(\texttt{revoked} 75.0\%, \texttt{emergency} 50.0\%). Statute B's
pre-registered per-atom predictions test this taxonomy: 5 of 7 hold
(artifact atoms \texttt{allocation\_granted} 38.3\% and \texttt{over\_quota}
46.1\% collapse; scenario atom \texttt{grant\_suspended} 72.7\% survives;
the epistemic-bar atom behaves as predicted), but \emph{two scenario-typed
atoms collapse anyway} (\texttt{contention} 38.0\%,
\texttt{essential\_workload} 28.8\%). The misses are systematic: both are
\emph{system-state} predicates whose intension asserts a fact about the
whole world ("the pool cannot serve all entitled demand"; "the harm cannot
reasonably be averted otherwise") rather than about an exhibited artifact or
event. The corrected lesson: the dominant axis is not subject matter but
\emph{verifiability at application time} --- open texture bites artifact
extensions hardest, and unverifiable intensions hurt the open regime
wherever they occur.

\textbf{Literalism widens the gap.} The naive capability prediction --- that
stronger models close the rules-vs-standards gap --- is \emph{not} supported,
and the truth is more interesting: the strongest reasoning model (gpt-5.4)
has the lowest or second-lowest closed score on both statutes (55.4\%,
64.4\%) and the largest gap on A ($+24.6$) and second-largest on B
($+18.2$). Reasoning models read enumerations most literally; literalism
makes rules more brittle. The same literalism punishes unverifiable
intensions: gpt-5.4 grounds open \texttt{emergency} at 9\% and
\texttt{anonymized} at 10\% recall on statute A (pooled over tiers), and
open \texttt{contention} at 11\% Tier-2 recall on statute B (near-zero false
positives in all cases) --- intensions that demand an unobservable guarantee
are refused by a strict reader, Kaplow's verification-cost condition visible
per-atom, replicated across domains.

\textbf{Repair is local --- and redrafting-by-example re-imports closure.}
One round of \emph{evidentiary redrafting} (re-stating pathological
intensions to judge described process and claim rather than unobservable
fact) lifts statute A's gpt-5.4 recall on the two bar atoms to 22\%/39\% and
flattens its atom curve at 82.1/81.7/81.8\%; on statute B the same operation
lifts \texttt{essential\_workload} from 40/38/55 to 52/62/70 across tiers.
But statute B also exposes the operation's failure mode: the
\texttt{offset\_posted} redraft named example forms (``depositing reserve
capacity, a confirmed top-up purchase\dots''), which raised Tier-0 recall
62$\to$92 while \emph{cutting} Tier-2 recall 52$\to$32 --- the strict reader
treats examples as a closed list. Evidentiary redrafting must state evidence
\emph{kinds} (recorded, escrowed, sized-to-cover), not instance forms: the
rules-vs-standards trade is fractal, reappearing inside the repair operation
itself. Repair remains local, iterative, and measurable --- a one-line
description edit with bounded blast radius, an operation a holistic judge
does not possess (pilot-stage precedent accretion on the closed side: 18/22
held-out misses fixed, zero in-distribution regressions; Appendix~F).

\subsection{RQ4: What the formal layer buys --- and what it honestly costs}

\textbf{Guarantees.} In every engine-mediated arm of every run on both
statutes, the deduction layer is exact by construction: the oracle arm
reproduces gold on all 1{,}152 oracle rows, so \emph{every error in every
grounded arm is a grounding error} --- an architectural guarantee that
isolates the layer the benchmark measures. Across paraphrase pairs, every
LLM arm flips 19--47\% of verdicts; \emph{program} and \emph{oracle} flip
0\%: the formal layer contributes zero variance, which is what makes
atom-level precedent caching (freeze an interpretation once made) the
natural determinism mechanism. On statute A's acted subset, grounded-closed
arms attribute violation and the notify remedy at 80--91\%/87--93\% versus
holistic's 71--83\%/68--78\% --- the breach-without-notify-duty distinction
the engine computes for free is precisely where holistic prose reasoning
slips (statute B: grounded 72--88\% on the report duty vs holistic
75--85\%, with the program arm itself degrading to 75/79 --- under-inclusive
grounding misfires the remedy chain too).

\textbf{Adversarial argument: the persuasion probe.} The factorization's
robustness claim --- a holistic judge can be argued with, a grounder
answering narrow membership questions presents a smaller persuasion surface,
and the engine cannot be argued with at all --- is testable, and
Table~\ref{tab:sway} tests it. The result is sharper than the prediction:
persuasion resistance is \emph{capability-dependent}. For the strongest
model the grounded arms are nearly immune (gpt-5.4: 0\%/4.3\% attack success
vs 12.0\% holistic; under advocacy its judgments even grow slightly more
conservative), but for weak models rhetoric penetrates at the atom layer
itself (deepseek grounded 17--18\%, \emph{worse} than its holistic):
narrowing the question immunizes only an interpreter strong enough to hold
the question narrow. Two scoring notes the probe surfaced: raw accuracy
under advocacy is the wrong metric (the advocate's factual recap helps weak
models, and pro-outcome pressure pushes toward the majority verdict class,
so several arms' accuracy \emph{rises} under attack --- the conditioned
attack-success rate is the honest number), and oracle/program at exactly
0\% confirm that all sway is interpretation-layer.

\textbf{Costs --- the conversion tax.} The guarantee above is bought at the
predicate layer; converting flat atom accuracy into verdicts pays a
compounding tax ($\approx \bar a^{\,k}$ over the $k$ flip-relevant atoms),
and on statute A the two strongest models do \emph{better} reading the whole
English statute holistically than through naive batched grounding of the
same open definitions (gpt-5.4: 83/73/75 vs 42/38/44; qwen: 87/72/78 vs
73/64/70; Tier-2 majority baseline 41.7\%). Statute B bounds the finding:
there, grounded-open meets or beats holistic at Tier 2 for three of four
families (gpt-4.1 $+15.6$, qwen $+5.2$, deepseek $-4.2$, gpt-5.4 $-13.5$
points) --- with fewer flip-relevant atoms per case the tax shrinks. The
honest summary: the conversion tax is real, capability- and
domain-dependent, and not yet engineered away; the \emph{conversion layer}
--- engine-guided criticality, burden-of-proof defaults, precedent caching
--- not the predicate layer, is where the engineering frontier sits (naive
per-atom staged grounding is already ruled out; pilot). A control matters
here: re-rendering cases as outcome-scrubbed tribunal FACTS lifts \emph{all}
arms uniformly ($+4.9$ to $+5.6$), so the holistic advantage is not
narration genre leakage.

\textbf{The conversion tax can reverse on deep structure.} If the tax is the
cost of the engine resolving structure the holistic judge resolves in prose,
its sign should depend on whether the judge can hold that structure --- and
the depth-5 agency statute (statute D) provides a case where it cannot. The
clearest instance is qwen: holistic-competitive on the 8-rule statutes
(78\%/76\% Tier-2) but \textbf{collapsing to 48.9\% --- the binary chance
baseline --- on the deep statute}, while its grounded pipeline holds at
80.4\% (a $+32$-point swing in the factorization's favor); it can no longer
resolve a five-deep exception chain in prose. The effect is decisive for
qwen alone: gpt-4.1 and deepseek are within noise either way ($-3$, $-2$),
and the strongest model \emph{reverses} the sign --- gpt-5.4 holistic holds
80\% while its own grounded verdict is only 62\% ($+18$ for holistic), the
same capability-exception as the literalism and persuasion findings. So the
honest claim is conditional, not universal: the grounded arm's per-atom task
is size-invariant by construction, but its \emph{verdict} accuracy is not
(more flip-relevant atoms per case means compounding bites the grounded arm
too, which is why gpt-5.4 grounded falls to 62\%); what statute D adds is a
single sharp existence proof that for a model that loses the thread on deep
structure, factoring the deduction out into a verified engine rescues verdict
accuracy rather than taxing it. One statute, one collapsing model: a
direction, not yet a curve --- the size axis is the clearest open call for
the suite expansion (Limitations).

\textbf{A second bearer, audited for free.} Statute D directs conduct duties
to the sub-agent (a disclose-then-disgorge chain) while the binding question
concerns the principal --- a distinction the engine computes from the
grounded atoms and a holistic verdict cannot separately express. Grounded
arms attribute the sub-agent's breach at 86--96\% across families; the no-LLM
program manages only 48\% (its under-inclusion misses the very predicates
that trigger the duty), and the holistic arm produces no separable sub-agent
verdict at all. The directed-obligation machinery yields a per-bearer audit
the unfactored judge structurally lacks.

\section{Limitations}

\textbf{Statute coverage and a failed prediction.} Results come from six
statutes spanning five deontic skeletons and the five institutions an agent
economy must govern (Appendix~B), but all are synthetic and authored by one
hand, and the suite is small relative to real legal diversity. We pre-%
registered each statute's texture profile: most held (D's small gap, F's
negative gap), but statute~E's predicted ``large'' gap \emph{failed} (it came
out near zero). The diagnosis is itself a result (RQ3): E's two quality
standards (\texttt{conforming}, \texttt{merchantable}) are \emph{relational}
(item vs.\ order-spec) and ungroundable in both regimes, while its other
closed enumerations are abstract enough to generalize --- so the open$>$closed
gap needs both a groundable predicate and a genuinely instance-specific
enumeration, not merely an ``artifact-type'' domain. We report the failed
prediction rather than dropping the statute; it is why the aggregate gap is
modest and why the verdict-layer factorization result, not the texture gap, is
our robust headline.
\textbf{Gold-by-intension circularity.} The same hand wrote the intensions,
the enumerations, and the banks. Mitigations: gold \emph{is} the
legislator's intension by design (the target regime), and two non-narrator
annotator families independently validated all 90 bank items (84/90, 87/90
agreement; all disagreements are strict-reader false negatives on the
epistemic-bar atoms, none touching the artifact-type Tier-2 items that drive
the headline gap; Appendix~E).
\textbf{Strawman risk.} The closed baselines are not straw: closed beats
open at Tier 0 for every family, and its scenario-type categories generalize
(emergency 50\%, revoked 75\% at Tier 2); the collapse is selective and
structural.
\textbf{Leakage and shared-narrator confound.} Lexical checks cannot exclude
paraphrastic leakage; the outcome-scrubbed control bounds genre effects but
not all composition artifacts, which are symmetric across regimes (the gap
is robust; absolute numbers are bounded by them). Moreover both statutes use
the same narrator family (and the same author wrote both statutes'
descriptions and banks), so any narrator-style or authorial artifact is
correlated across the two runs: the replication is evidence that the texture
effect transfers across domains, not independent evidence against narration
confounds. A third statute with a different narrator family and, ideally, a
second bank author is the right next control.
\textbf{Scope.} 7 atoms per statute, 3-class verdicts, single bearer, four
grounder families; frontier models may eventually saturate Tier 2 --- by
design that result would itself confirm the thesis (the application-time
interpreter got good enough). The persuasion probe is similarly scoped: one
statute, one advocacy direction (pro-permit), and verdict-level rhetoric
only --- atom-targeted characterization arguments (``this is not really
personal data because\dots''), the realistic lawyer's move, are the
stronger untested attack.

\section{Conclusion}

Law's open-textured predicates are not an obstacle to formalization but its
generalization mechanism: they defer concept-binding to application time.
LLMs make that deferred binding cheap --- we measure $\sim$\$0.002 per case
--- and a defeasible deontic skeleton keeps it consistent and auditable:
zero deduction errors, zero structural variance, replayable certificates,
violation and remedy semantics that holistic judges measurably fumble.
Factored this way, machine-readable norms generalize where fully-specified
rules cannot --- but conditionally, and the honest aggregate over six statutes
says where. At the predicate layer the program dies on the first unlisted
instance, the extensional curve decays with novelty while the intensional one
holds flat (the rules-vs-standards crossover, pooled), \emph{when} the
predicate is groundable and its extension genuinely instance-specific; where
the intension is an unverifiable guarantee the advantage reverses. The
robust, unconditional result is at the verdict layer: the factored
architecture --- grounding under a verified engine --- beats both the no-LLM
program and the unfactored holistic judge on every statute in the suite,
because the engine resolves exception structure that prose reasoning fumbles.
The benchmark pipeline that establishes this
--- engine-verified gold, controlled novelty, contamination excluded by
construction --- is reusable for any normative domain expressible as a
defeasible theory, and we release it with the engine and all artifacts. For
the governance of AI agent societies the lesson is Kaplow's, updated:
ex-ante drafting cannot buy coverage of the unforeseen, ex-post
interpretation now can --- so write short intensions, verify the deduction,
and let interpretation accrete into precedent. What remains open is the
conversion layer --- turning flat predicate accuracy into verdict accuracy
without the compounding tax --- and the architecture points at its own
remedy: the engine knows each case's flip set, burdens of proof are
defeasible-logic natives, and cached interpretations are case law.

\section{Appendix A: The Statute and the DDL Language}

\paragraph{Reading \texttt{.ddl}.} A theory is a set of \emph{atoms} (opaque
tokens, each with a mandatory natural-language description --- the atom's
contract), \emph{rules}, and a \emph{superiority relation}. Rules have the
form $body \Rightarrow_{O@Bearer} head$: \texttt{=>O@Holder} introduces an
obligation directed at the bearer \texttt{Holder} (strict arrow); the tilde
form \texttt{\textasciitilde>O} introduces a \emph{defeasible permission}
(the conclusion holds unless a superior rule defeats it); \texttt{\~{}share}
is negation (an obligation \emph{not} to share, i.e.\ a prohibition);
\texttt{*} chains compensatory obligations: $O\neg share \otimes notify
\otimes compensate$ reads ``share is forbidden; if shared anyway, notify is
obligatory; if notification also fails, compensate.'' \texttt{r1 > r0} means
r1 defeats r0 when both fire. The engine implements
Governatori-style defeasible deontic proof theory
\citep{governatori2018practical,governatori2024oddity} in Lean~4; verdicts
carry replayable proof certificates (\texttt{--why}).

\paragraph{The statute.} Eight rules over seven groundable atoms
(\texttt{personal\_data}, \texttt{consent}, \texttt{revoked},
\texttt{emergency}, \texttt{anonymized}, \texttt{commercial},
\texttt{certified}):

\begin{lstlisting}
r0: ~>O@Holder share
r1: personal_data =>O@Holder ~share
    * notify * compensate
r2: consent ~>O@Holder share
r3: revoked =>O@Holder ~share
r4: emergency =>O@Holder share
r5: anonymized ~>O@Holder share
r6: commercial =>O@Holder ~share
r7: consent, certified ~>O@Holder share

superiority:
  r1>r0, r3>r0, r6>r0, r2>r1, r5>r1,
  r7>r1, r3>r2, r3>r7, r5>r3, r5>r6,
  r6>r2, r7>r6, r4>r1, r4>r3, r4>r6
\end{lstlisting}

Default permission to share (r0); default prohibition for personal data with
a two-step compensatory chain (r1); consent permits (r2) but revocation
defeats consent (r3 $>$ r2); anonymization moots even revocation (r5 $>$ r3);
emergencies create a duty to share that beats every prohibition layer (r4);
commercial exploitation is separately prohibited (r6) unless the recipient is
certified \emph{and} consent exists (r7). Breaches of r3/r6 carry no notify
duty --- only the r1 chain does --- giving the
violation-without-remedy distinction tested in RQ4. Verdict space: status of
\texttt{share}; on acted cases additionally \texttt{violation} and
\texttt{notify\_required}. The 56 coherent latent worlds arise from the
world-coherence constraints (revocation presupposes a grant; a grant and
de-identification presuppose person-level content).

\section{Appendix B: Atom Material --- Descriptions and Instance Banks}

Each atom carries: \texttt{open} (the intension; mirrors the statute and
defines gold), \texttt{closed} (the enumeration; 3--8 categories per atom,

\section{Appendix C: Generation Pipeline Details}

\textbf{Case grid.} 48 latent assignments $\times$ 3 tiers $\times$ 2
paraphrase variants $=$ 288 cases (statute B identical: 288 cases, 114
acted), stratified over the 56 coherent worlds; a separate 288-case
re-narration in tribunal ruling-FACTS style serves as the genre-leakage
control. 120 of statute A's cases are ``acted'' (the transfer already
happened), activating violation/remedy gold.
\textbf{Narrator isolation.} The narrator (claude-sonnet-4.6) is from a
third model family disjoint from all four grounder families; it sees the
instantiated facts, never the gold verdict (backward generation makes
narrations outcome-free by construction).
\textbf{Leak check.} A narration is rejected if it contains: any atom name;
statute vocabulary stems; any 5-gram of any open description; any 5-gram of
a closed description at Tiers 1--2 (Tier-0 instances echo drafted categories
by design). One case of 288 was flagged in the headline run and excluded
($n=287$).
\textbf{Grounding protocol.} Grounders receive the memo plus seven
definitions under neutral labels C1..C7 (no atom names, no statute), and
return a boolean per label; the regimes differ only in definition text.
Holistic arms receive the full English statute (with open or closed
definitions) plus the memo and return verdict/violation/remedy.
\textbf{Pre-registration.} Predictions P1--P5 were committed (git hash
\texttt{ab71c99}) before any LLM grounding or holistic run; the four Tier-2
gaps of Table~\ref{tab:gaps} are the confirmatory family.
\textbf{Cost.} Grounding: $\sim$650--780 output tokens/case for
OpenAI-family models ($\approx$\$0.002 at gpt-4.1 prices; deepseek emits
2--3$\times$ the tokens at $\sim$1/10 the unit price); staged per-atom
grounding is $\sim$4$\times$ dearer; holistic $\sim$870--2{,}270
tokens/case.

\section{Appendix F: The Audit Loop and Disclosed Interventions}

Three world-model defects were caught between pilot and headline run, each
with the signature \emph{grounder right, gold wrong}: (i) an ``anonymization
done badly'' distractor attached to a non-personal dataset made the narrator
imply quasi-identifiers gold denied; (ii) urgency items narrated as separate
requests correctly failed the open definition's necessity test --- emergency
banks were re-bound to the requested dataset; (iii) grounders inferred
personhood from consent evidence, exposing a missing world constraint (a
grant presupposes a data subject). Each was fixed in the generator and the
affected cases regenerated before the headline run.

One generation-time intervention is asymmetric and disclosed:
\texttt{anonymized} items are injected with explicit derivation framing
(``what leaves the holder is a transformed product derived from those
records''), because the narrator's keep-datasets-distinct rule otherwise
renders the transformation as a separate dataset, which grounders rightly
deny. Without the framing, open-regime anonymized recall is 12--13\%
(pilot); with it, 39--47\% (Stage 2). We read this as fixing narration
fidelity to the latent world, not tuning toward the thesis --- but the lever
exists and was applied to one atom only.

Pilot-stage repair experiments (archived with the pilot artifacts):
precedent accretion on the closed side --- appending decided Tier-2
instances from half the Tier-2 cases --- repaired 18/22 previously-missed
true atoms on held-out cases with 2 new breaks and zero Tier-0/1 verdict
regressions; evidentiary redrafting on the open side lifted deepseek
verdict accuracy 58$\to$75 (Tier 0). Both are one-line description edits
with measured blast radius: the maintenance operation the factorization
enables and a holistic judge lacks.

\section{Appendix G: The Commons Statute (Statute B)}

\section{Appendix H: The Park and Agency Statutes (C, D)}

\paragraph{Statute C --- ``no vehicles in the park'' (3 rules, 2 atoms).}

(Figure~\ref{fig:concept}). r0: entry permitted; r1: \texttt{vehicle}
$\Rightarrow$ forbidden; r2: \texttt{emergency} $\Rightarrow$ obligatory

\section{Appendix I: Statute Selection and Statutes E, F}

\paragraph{Selection rationale (three crossed axes).} The six statutes are
chosen so the generalization result cannot be an artifact of one domain,
texture, or size (full version: \texttt{eval/DOMAIN\_RATIONALE.md}).
\emph{Institutions}: one per coordination problem an agent economy must
govern --- information (A), resources (B), authority (D), exchange (E), safety
(F), with C as Hart's illustration. \emph{Texture}: pre-registered per-%
predicate types span artifact $\to$ scenario $\to$ epistemic-bar.
\emph{Size}: 3/8/8/9/11/12 rules. The aggregate (Figure~\ref{fig:agg},
Table~\ref{tab:aggverdict}) is then the designed average over these axes;
per-statute numbers are points on them. All runs share the QA gates
(\S Method); the description-edit protocol (edits only on blind bank-%
validation ambiguity, enumeration-free, validate-before-grade) is in
\texttt{eval/QA\_PROTOCOL.md}.

\paragraph{Statute E --- sale of goods (exchange).} 9 rules, 9 atoms, depth-4
exception chain; binding question @Buyer \texttt{accept} (must the buyer
accept and pay?), remedy reject-notice $\otimes$ refund, seller cure.
Predicates are quality/conformity standards (\texttt{conforming},
\texttt{merchantable}, \texttt{material\_defect}, \texttt{fitness\_breached}).
Pre-registered ``large gap'' \emph{failed} ($-0.3$ at Tier 2): the two
quality standards are relational (item vs.\ order-spec) and ungroundable in
both regimes (closed recall 27\%, 37\% even at Tier 0), and the other closed
enumerations are abstract enough to generalize. The factorization result is
strong here --- grounded+engine holds $\approx$81--91\% verdict accuracy across
tiers vs holistic's 64--76\%. Independent bank validation (llama-3.3-70b,
qwen3.6-plus): 94.3\%, 95.3\%, no systematic ambiguity. Full:
\texttt{eval/expE/RESULTS.md}.

atoms; binding question @Agent \texttt{act}, remedy rollback $\otimes$
disclose. The epistemic-bar pole: gating predicates
(\texttt{irreversible}, \texttt{contained}, \texttt{averting\_harm},
\texttt{reversible\_window}) demand unverifiable guarantees, so the
\emph{open} intension is hard for a strict reader to affirm --- open atom
recall is \emph{depressed below} closed (77/80/78 vs 88/85/83), the Tier-2 gap
\emph{reverses} ($-5.4$). Where the intension is unverifiable, the standard
loses to the operational rule (Kaplow's verification-cost condition at
statute scale). grounded-closed verdict accuracy 87/78/84 clears holistic
60/67/57; program collapses 100/33/33. Independent bank validation
(llama-3.3-70b, qwen3.6-plus): 96.5\%, 93.0\%, no systematic ambiguity. This
statute is the Constitutional-AI%
-relevant one (\S RQ2 discussion): a safety constitution whose exception
structure the engine resolves. Full: \texttt{eval/expF/RESULTS.md}.

### Background to the appeals (First Appeal)

This case concerns two consolidated appeals. The First Appeal concerns IC decision IC-292124-F1N2 dated 28 October 2024, arising from Mr Curtin's request for information submitted to the Ministry of Defence (MOD) on 2 November 2023 (the "November Request"), which was itself a refinement of his August 2023 request (the "August Request") about Unidentified Aerial or Anomalous Phenomena (UAPs) and/or Unidentified Flying Objects (UFOs).

Mr Curtin first wrote to the MOD on 31 August 2023 seeking, for the period from 1 January 2009 to the current date: documents, reports, photographs or memos related to UAP/UFO; documents related to the Five Eyes Foreign Material Recovery Program (FMP) with a specific focus on UAP/UFO; information on the FMP's objective, scope and operation regarding UAP; assessments or analyses regarding recovered foreign materials associated with UAP/UFO; communications between the UK government and other Five Eyes members regarding the FMP and UAP/UFO; and records of encounters with UAP/UFO including incident reports and analysis of potential national security threats.

The MOD refused the August Request on 26 September 2023 under section 12 (cost limit), explaining that it would exceed the 24-hour appropriate limit. The MOD provided clarification on 2 November 2023 including a breakdown showing that a search using key terms from the request on one MOD internal network yielded 20,020 links related to "national security", "threats" and "Five Eyes", and that allocating 3 minutes per link would take 138 days — well in excess of the cost limit.

On 2 November 2023, Mr Curtin submitted the November Request as a refinement, seeking to reduce the search by removing "national security" and "threat" terms, and focusing on: Five Eyes (FVEY) or Five Eyes Material Programme (FMP); Unidentified Flying Objects (UFO); Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena (UAP); Unidentified Aerial Phenomena (UAP); and All-Domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO). He also offered further refinements if needed.

The MOD responded on 4 December 2023, treating the November Request as a stand-alone request and refusing to confirm or deny (NCND) that it held information in scope of the request, citing sections 23 (security bodies), 24 (national security), 26 (defence) and 27 (international relations). The MOD did not apply section 12. Mr Curtin requested an internal review; the MOD upheld its NCND response on 29 February 2024.

The IC issued the First Decision on 28 October 2024, finding that the MOD was entitled to rely on section 23(5) and section 27(4) of FOIA to refuse to confirm or deny that it holds the requested information. Mr Curtin appealed on 11 November 2024.

At the hearing, the MOD applied (by GRC5 form dated 9 January 2026) to submit evidence that, if the August and November Requests were treated as one whole request, the correct response would have been section 12. The Tribunal admitted this late evidence in the interests of justice. The Tribunal conducted an open session and a closed session (attended only by MOD and IC representatives); a gist of the closed session was provided to Mr Curtin.

The relevant legal framework: Section 12(1) provides that section 1(1) does not oblige a public authority to comply with a request if the authority estimates that the cost of complying would exceed the appropriate limit. For the MOD, the appropriate limit is £600 (24 hours at £25/hour). The Tribunal's role under section 58 FOIA is to consider whether the IC's decision was in accordance with the law, applying the law afresh to the request.

## Disputed information

Documents, reports, photographs, memos, and other recorded information relating to UAP/UFO and/or the Five Eyes Foreign Material Recovery Program (FMP) held by the Ministry of Defence from 1 January 2009 to November 2023, as refined by the November Request to focus on the search terms: Five Eyes (FVEY)/FMP, UFO, UAP, and AARO (excluding "national security" and "threats").

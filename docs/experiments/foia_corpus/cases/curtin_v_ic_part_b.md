### Background to the appeals (Second Appeal)

This case concerns two consolidated appeals. The Second Appeal concerns IC decision IC-348013-F2L6 dated 17 March 2025, arising from Mr Curtin's request for information submitted to the Ministry of Defence (MOD) on 21 August 2024.

Mr Curtin wrote to the MOD on 21 August 2024 requesting all details in all formats (notes, presentation slides, videos, etc.) relating to the United Kingdom UAP update section only, from the FVEY Inaugural UAP Caucus Working Group meeting held at the Pentagon on 24 May 2023, including information provided by the two MOD officials and any questions they received and responses they gave. This request was prompted by public disclosures by the US government (published 31 May 2023) and the MOD itself (2 August 2024) that two MOD officials attended the meeting.

The MOD responded on 17 September 2024, applying an NCND (neither confirm nor deny) response under sections 23(5), 24(2), 26(3) and 27(4). On 4 October 2024, following Mr Curtin's request for informal review, the MOD clarified that since US disclosure of information, the NCND principle had changed for most exemptions — but s23(5) NCND still applied. On further formal review (24 January 2025), the MOD reverted fully to NCND under all four exemptions.

The IC issued the Second Decision on 17 March 2025, finding that the MOD was entitled to rely on section 23(5) alone to issue an NCND response.

Mr Curtin appealed on 30 March 2025. He argued that the IC failed to give enough weight to the government's own 'non-threat' policy position on UAPs, failed to adequately investigate the other exemptions (ss 24, 26, 27), and that the MOD's inconsistent application of NCND (having itself released information about the meeting) undermined the s23(5) reliance.

The IC responded maintaining that section 23(5) is a class-based absolute exemption (not dependent on government policy or information in the public domain), and that once it was satisfied, the IC was not required to consider other exemptions. The Tribunal confirmed it has no power to review the method or scope of the IC's investigation, nor to sanction delays. The UNUDHR Article 19 argument was rejected as unincorporated international law outside the Tribunal's FOIA jurisdiction (following Moss).

Section 23(5) FOIA provides that the duty to confirm or deny does not arise if compliance with section 1(1)(a) would involve disclosure of any information (whether or not already recorded) which was directly or indirectly supplied by, or relates to, the bodies specified in section 23(3) (including the Security Service, SIS, GCHQ, and special forces). Section 23 is an absolute exemption: once engaged, no public interest balancing is required.

## Disputed information

Whether the Ministry of Defence will confirm or deny that it holds the UK's presentation materials and related records from the FVEY Inaugural UAP Caucus Working Group meeting held at the Pentagon on 24 May 2023, specifically the UK's UAP update section including notes, slides, and records of questions and answers involving MOD officials.

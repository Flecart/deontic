### Introduction

1. This is an appeal against Decision Notice referenced IC-297382-LB52 dated 17 September 2024.

2. The Appellant requested information from Mid Devon District Council ("the Council") relating to the Council's leisure charges strategy. The Council's leisure services are offered to the community through three leisure centres owned by the Council in the Mid Devon area and operated at the material time by the Council's leisure services arm called Mid Devon Leisure ("MDL").

3. By the Decision Notice, the Respondent ("the Commissioner") decided that the Council was entitled to withhold the requested information pursuant to s43(2) (commercial interests) of the Freedom of Information Act 2000 ("FOIA"), and that the Council was not required to take any further steps.

4. The Tribunal's decision is unanimous.

5. The Tribunal has issued an OPEN decision of 44 pages, and a CLOSED decision of 63 pages addressing in detail the information requested by the Appellant which the Council contends should be withheld.

### Background

6. The Council commissioned a report from Max Associates Limited, a specialist leisure consultancy, following a July 2023 Council resolution to review leisure pricing. The report was procured at a cost of almost £20,000 and contained detailed market analysis, financial information, demographic analysis, and strategic recommendations for Mid Devon Leisure's operations.

7. On 12 January 2024, the Appellant requested the Max Associates Report (among other things). The Council refused disclosure citing s43(2) FOIA.

8. The Commissioner upheld the refusal in his Decision Notice dated 17 September 2024.

### The Legal Framework

49. The relevant provisions of FOIA are as follows: Section 43 Commercial Interests ... (2) Information is exempt information if its disclosure under this Act would, or would be likely to, prejudice the commercial interests of any person (including the public authority holding it).

### Max Associates Report — sections with s43(2) engaged and withheld

87. The tribunal determined that a small part of the Max Associates Report is exempt from disclosure pursuant to s43(2) FOIA. The reasoning for the specific portions is set out in CLOSED.

104. For the reasons set out in CLOSED, the tribunal concluded that disclosure of some but not all of Section 4 of the Max Associates Report would be likely to prejudice the Council's commercial interests. Accordingly, s43(2) FOIA is engaged in part.

106. For the reasons set out in CLOSED, the tribunal concluded that disclosure of some but not all of Section 5 of the Max Associates Report would be likely to prejudice the Council's commercial interests. Accordingly, s43(2) FOIA is engaged in part.

113. For the reasons set out in CLOSED, the tribunal concluded that disclosure of some but not all of Section 9 of the Max Associates Report would be likely to prejudice the Council's commercial interests. Accordingly, s43(2) FOIA is engaged in part.

114–123. Where s43(2) is engaged, the tribunal considered the public interest balance. For the specific data identified in CLOSED (within sections 4, 5 and 9) where the risk of prejudice was found not to be low, the tribunal found the public interest in maintaining the exemption outweighed the public interest in disclosure.

## Disputed information

Specific portions of sections 4, 5, and 9 of the Max Associates Report (a specialist leisure consultancy report commissioned by Mid Devon District Council). The exact content of these portions is set out in the tribunal's CLOSED decision and relates to detailed market analysis, financial data, and strategic recommendations the tribunal found would be likely to prejudice the Council's commercial interests if disclosed. This is the small part of the Max Associates Report that the Council is entitled to withhold.

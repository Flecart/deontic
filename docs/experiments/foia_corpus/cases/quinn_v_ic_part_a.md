### Introduction

1. This is an appeal against Decision Notice referenced IC-297382-LB52 dated 17 September 2024.

2. The Appellant requested information from Mid Devon District Council ("the Council") relating to the Council's leisure charges strategy. The Council's leisure services are offered to the community through three leisure centres owned by the Council in the Mid Devon area and operated at the material time by the Council's leisure services arm called Mid Devon Leisure ("MDL").

3. By the Decision Notice, the Respondent ("the Commissioner") decided that the Council was entitled to withhold the requested information pursuant to s43(2) (commercial interests) of the Freedom of Information Act 2000 ("FOIA"), and that the Council was not required to take any further steps.

4. The Tribunal's decision is unanimous.

5. The Tribunal has issued an OPEN decision of 44 pages, and a CLOSED decision of 63 pages addressing in detail the information requested by the Appellant which the Council contends should be withheld.

### Background

6. At a Council meeting on 19 July 2023, the Councillors and Officers of the Council moved a resolution to review the pricing of the Council's leisure services. We set out in full the relevant extract from the minutes of that meeting: "Motion: Leisure Services VAT 1. That this Council is pleased at the outcome of the legal challenge which has concluded in March that local authority leisure services are to be treated as nonbusiness for VAT purposes and welcomes the Leisure VAT refund of almost £3M now due from HMRC to support the past and continued subsidisation of the Council's Leisure Services. 2. This Council asks that a report be prepared for the appropriate Policy Development Group to consider the various issues and options available and then make recommendations to Cabinet and Council in the usual way in relation to the budget. 3. That the Council should consider changes to the Leisure Services Pricing structure after a full review and recommendation from the relevant PDG. 4. That this council should fully review all leisure pricing in an open and transparent way by conducting a review of pricing through the appropriate Policy Development Group before making recommendations to Cabinet in the usual way for implementation from 1st April 2024 (2024-2025) financial year. And that the review should have due regard for the subsidy given to the leisure services and the impact on the wider district Taxpayer of any reduction."

7. After the Council meeting on 19 July 2023, the Council commissioned a report from Max Associates Limited, a specialist leisure consultancy.

8. On 24 October 2023, the Council's Community Policy Development Group met to consider proposals to change the Council's leisure charges strategy.

9. On 14 November 2023, the Cabinet meeting of the Council approved the recommended changes to the leisure charges.

10. On 1 January 2024, the Council's new leisure service charges were implemented.

11. On 12 January 2024, the Appellant sent a request to the Council seeking: (a) the Max Associates Report (the report prepared by the specialist leisure consultancy); (b) the PDG Report (the report presented to the Community Policy Development Group on 24 October 2023); and (c) the 2024 Leisure Charges Publication (details of what was published about the new charges, when, where, and for how long).

12. The Council refused to disclose the Max Associates Report and PDG Report under s43(2) FOIA, and directed the Appellant to the Cabinet agenda papers in response to the 2024 Leisure Charges Publication request, citing s21 FOIA.

13. The Commissioner upheld the Council's refusal in his Decision Notice dated 17 September 2024.

### The Legal Framework

49. The relevant provisions of FOIA are as follows: Section 43 Commercial Interests ... (2) Information is exempt information if its disclosure under this Act would, or would be likely to, prejudice the commercial interests of any person (including the public authority holding it).

### S43(2) FOIA Commercial interests

51. S43 FOIA is a prejudice-based exemption. The approach requires: (a) identification of the applicable interests; (b) a causal link between disclosure and prejudice that is real, actual or of substance; (c) the degree of risk must be such that there is a "real and significant" risk of prejudice.

### PDG Report — finding

64–85. The tribunal examined the PDG Report. In OPEN, the tribunal found that all the withheld information in the PDG Report (save for a small item of personal data) should be disclosed: either s43(2) is not engaged, or where it is engaged, the public interest in maintaining the exemption is outweighed by the public interest in disclosure (para 68). The tribunal found the Commissioner erred in deciding the Council was entitled to withhold the PDG Report (para 84).

### Max Associates Report — partially disclose

87–125. The tribunal examined the Max Associates Report section by section. It found that s43(2) is NOT engaged for: the title page; sections 1 (introduction and most of the table of contents), 2, 3, 6, 7, and 8. For sections 4, 5 and 9, s43(2) is engaged in part. For those partially-engaged parts, the tribunal found the public interest in maintaining the exemption was outweighed by the public interest in disclosure for most of the material (paras 114–123). The Commissioner erred in deciding the Council was entitled to withhold all of the Max Associates Report (para 124).

### 2024 Leisure Charges Publication

126–136. The Appellant sought the publication of the new 2024 Leisure Charges: what was actually published, when, where, and for how long. The tribunal found the Council wrong to refuse to disclose this information. The Council remained bound to answer this part of the request in compliance with s1(1) and s17(1) FOIA (para 134).

## Disputed information

The PDG Report (the report presented to the Community Policy Development Group on 24 October 2023 on the Council's leisure pricing strategy for 2024/25), most of the Max Associates Report (sections 1–3, 6–8 entirely, and the non-exempt portions of sections 4, 5, and 9), and details of the 2024 Leisure Charges Publication (what was published, when, where, and for how long, including any prior notification of the new charges). The tribunal ordered all of this to be disclosed: s43(2) was not engaged for most of it, and where it was engaged the public interest balance favoured disclosure.

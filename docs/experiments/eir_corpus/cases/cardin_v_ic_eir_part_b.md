### (preamble)

1. This is an appeal against a decision of the Information Commissioner ("the Commissioner") dated 21 May 2025, reference IC-332330-S7R4 ("the Decision Notice").

2. The parties opted for a paper determination of the appeal. The Tribunal is satisfied that it can properly determine the issues without a hearing in accordance with rule 2 and rule 32(1)(b) of The Tribunal Procedure (First-tier Tribunal)(General Regulatory Chamber) Rules 2009 (as amended).

### Factual background

3. The appeal relates to the application of the Environmental Information Regulations 2004 ("the EIR"). The relevant Public Authority is the Royal Borough of Kingston Upon Thames ("the Council").

### The request and response

4. The Appellant made the request which is the subject of this appeal on 15 July 2024 in writing as follows: "I noticed that there is at least one potentially fraudulent ICNIRP safety certificate contained in Kingston upon Thames Borough Council's planning portal, an online facility which displays details of planning applications and approvals. A company named 'Three UK Limited' – which was dissolved in the year 2015 – has somehow become the signatory for at least one bogus safety certificate (sometimes referred to as a conformity statement) for a 5G telecommunications mast installation. Using your best and most honest endeavours, please provide: 1. A figure for the precise number of ICNIRP safety certificates related to 5G telecommunications mast installations that have been signed off by 'Three UK Limited', a company that didn't exist at the time the relevant planning applications were made, and whose declared ICNIRP safety certificates are potentially fraudulent. […] 3. The actual dates of the initial planning applications made for 5G telecommunications mast installations that have been allegedly ICNIRP 'safety certified' by bogus certificates, along with the named street locations and identifying nomenclature. 4. The time period, stated in years, months, weeks and days, between the date of the dissolution of 'Three UK Limited'(27th October 2015) and the date of the initial applications made for all of the 5G telecommunications masts concerned, having bogus safety certificates […] 5. […] please provide detailed information explaining how Dear Kingston upon Thames Borough Council is planning to restore council tax payers, voters, their children, and Dear Kingston upon Thames Borough Council's wildlife and domestic pets back to a safe, non-hazardous environment […]"

5. On 16 July 2024, a Planning Link Officer from the Council emailed the Appellant to ask for the site location address of the mast that the Appellant asserted had a potentially fraudulent ICNIRP safety certificate. The Appellant provided the address of the mast on 21 July 2024.

6. The Council responded to Parts 1, 3, 4 and 5 of the Appellant's request on 12 August 2024: "[Part One] We have considered this request Under Environmental Information Regulations (EIR) and we have determined that the information should be withheld (if it exists) under exception 12(4)(b) due to being 'manifestly unreasonable'. To explain, the council would need to search the planning database on the council website for several keywords, reading the documents to determine if they meet the scope of the request, and if the requested information is held. Due to the volume of records, this would place an excessive time burden on the council. […] [Part Three] Please see the answer to question 1 above with regards to planning approval documents. [Part Four] The information on 'time periods' would not be held by the council, and is therefore refused under EIR exception 12(4)(a) 'Information not held'. […] [Part Five] The information on 'WHO' would not be held by the council, and is therefore refused under EIR exception 12(4)(a) 'Information not held'. This is not a request for recorded information held by the Council."

8. The Council's internal review on 05 September 2024 maintained reg 12(4)(b) for Parts 1 and 3: "Having reviewed your original request, I am of the view that the exception applied under EIR regulation 12(4)(b) 'Manifestly Unreasonable' with regard to 'a figure for the precise number of ICNIRP safety certificates related to 5G telecommunications mast installations that have been signed off by Three UK Limited' (Q1/Q2) was appropriate and remains withheld under this exception. This is because we have calculated that it would place an excessive, disproportionate and unreasonable burden on the service and ultimately the Council to respond to your request."

### The complaint and the Commissioner's investigation

9. The Appellant lodged a complaint with the Commissioner on 16 September 2024.

17. The Council maintained reg 12(4)(b) in their response to the Commissioner: "Each application would need to be read in order to determine whether or not it contained the information requested. This is because the information may be embedded within a planning application and would not be a stand alone document that could easily be searched. We have calculated that this is likely to take an officer at least 103 working hours to complete which we consider to be unreasonable as it would take the officer away from carrying out his/her core duties for a considerable time. This figure equates to approximately 6000 applications at 15 min each."

30. Whilst reviewing the matter upon receiving the Appellant's appeal, the Commissioner noted that the Council had provided incorrect figures: the correct total was approximately 1547.5 hours (6190 applications at 15 minutes each), which the Council confirmed on 18 June 2025.

### Decision notice

21. The Commissioner's decision was that the Council was entitled to rely on regulation 12(4)(b) to withhold information in scope of Parts 1 and 3 of the request and that the public interest favoured maintaining the exception; and that, on the balance of probabilities, the Council did not hold information within the scope of Parts 4 and 5 of the request.

23. In relation to reg 12(4)(b), the Commissioner considered that the Council had demonstrated why it would cause a significant burden to respond, based on searching approximately 6,000 applications at a rate of one per 15 minutes.

24. In relation to the public interest test, the Commissioner concluded that the public interest in maintaining the exception outweighed the public interest in disclosure.

25. In relation to reg 12(4)(a) for Parts 4 and 5, the Commissioner held that the requested information was not held by the Council.

### Grounds of appeal

27. The Appellant lodged his appeal on 21 May 2025.

28. The Appellant relied chiefly on the fact that Sefton Borough Council had responded in full to the same request, arguing this demonstrated the request was not manifestly unreasonable. He sought "a positive outcome whereby the information is released".

### The response of the Commissioner

33. The Commissioner lodged his response on 26 June 2025, in which he maintained that the Decision Notice was correct and that the appeal should be dismissed.

34. The Commissioner's response noted: the corrected estimate of 1547.5 hours "firmly solidifies that his decision is correct"; the Appellant had not challenged the timings put forward by the Council; and comparisons with other councils were not meaningful as each is a different public authority with different resources.

35. The Commissioner also noted that, had the request been handled under FOIA, the publicly-accessible nature of the planning portal might have provided grounds for refusal under s.21 FOIA, and that the Appellant's ability to search the portal themselves was a factor weighing the public interest in favour of maintaining the exception.

### Legal Framework

47. A public authority may refuse to disclose information to the extent that the request for information is manifestly unreasonable and where, in all of the circumstances of the case, the public interest in maintaining the exception outweighs the public interest in disclosing the information.

48. This exception can apply if the cost or burden of dealing with a request is too great. The inclusion of the word "manifestly" means that there must be an obvious or clear quality to the unreasonableness.

50. The Freedom of Information and Data Protection (Appropriate Limit and Fees) Regulations 2004 are not directly applicable to EIR but give a useful indication that Parliament considered 18 hours to be a reasonable allocation of resources for a public authority.

51. The public interest in maintaining the exception lies in protecting public authorities from exposure to disproportionate burden. The correct approach is to identify and balance the public interests in disclosure and withholding.

### The role of the Tribunal

53. By virtue of regulation 18 of the EIR, the Tribunal's remit is governed by section 58 of FOIA. This requires the Tribunal to consider whether the decision made by the Commissioner is in accordance with the law.

### Issues

54. The issues for the Tribunal include whether the Commissioner was correct in holding that the Council was entitled to rely on regulation 12(4)(b) of the EIR to refuse Parts 1 and 3 of the request and, if so, whether the public interest balance favoured disclosure or withholding; and whether reg 12(4)(a) was correctly applied to Parts 4 and 5.

### Evidence

55. We read and took account of an open bundle containing 102 pages including indexes.

## Disputed information

Parts 1 and 3 of the request: (1) a precise count of ICNIRP safety certificates related to 5G telecommunications mast installations on the Council's planning portal signed off by "Three UK Limited", and (3) the dates, street locations, and identifying nomenclature of the initial planning applications for those installations. The Council withheld on reg 12(4)(b) (manifestly unreasonable) on the basis that searching approximately 6,190 planning applications at 15 minutes each would require approximately 1,547 hours of officer time. Also in dispute (Parts 4 and 5): derivation of time periods between Three UK Ltd's dissolution and planning application dates, and a remediation plan for alleged health risks — refused on reg 12(4)(a) (not held).

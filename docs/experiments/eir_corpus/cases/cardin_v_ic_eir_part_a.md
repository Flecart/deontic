### (preamble)

1. This is an appeal against a decision of the Information Commissioner ("the Commissioner") dated 21 May 2025, reference IC-332330-S7R4 ("the Decision Notice").

2. The parties opted for a paper determination of the appeal. The Tribunal is satisfied that it can properly determine the issues without a hearing in accordance with rule 2 and rule 32(1)(b) of The Tribunal Procedure (First-tier Tribunal)(General Regulatory Chamber) Rules 2009 (as amended).

### Factual background

3. The appeal relates to the application of the Environmental Information Regulations 2004 ("the EIR"). The relevant Public Authority is the Royal Borough of Kingston Upon Thames ("the Council").

### The request and response

4. The Appellant made the request which is the subject of this appeal on 15 July 2024 in writing as follows: "[…] 2. Actual copies of the ICNIRP safety certificates related to 5G telecommunications mast installations that have been signed off by 'Three UK Limited', a company that didn't exist at the time of the planning applications, and are therefore potentially fraudulent. […]"

5. On 16 July 2024, a Planning Link Officer from the Council emailed the Appellant to ask for the site location address of the mast that the Appellant asserted had a potentially fraudulent ICNIRP safety certificate. The Appellant provided the address of the mast on 21 July 2024.

6. The Council responded to Part 2 of the Appellant's request on 12 August 2024 as follows: "[Part Two] The information on 'copies of the ICNIRP safety certificates' would not be held by the council, and is therefore refused under EIR exception 12(4)(a) 'Information not held'. We would recommend that you direct this question to the mast owner(s)/operator(s)."

7. The Appellant submitted a request for an internal review on 17 August 2024.

8. The Council's internal review on 05 September 2024 maintained reg 12(4)(a) for Part 2: "With regard to questions 2, 4 and 5, the exception applied under EIR 12(4)(a) 'Information not Held' continues to apply as the Council does not hold the information requested".

### The complaint and the Commissioner's investigation

9. The Appellant lodged a complaint with the Commissioner on 16 September 2024.

15. In response to Commissioner's questions (05 February 2025), the Council stated: "A limited number of searches of the planning database, (electronic records) were carried out by Planning colleagues. Each search took approximately 15 mins to look through one application and search each document to determine if any 'ICNCRP safety certificates' were held by the Council. Two were located but a nil return in the remaining applications searched."

### Decision notice

25. In relation to the application of regulation 12(4)(a) of the EIR, the Commissioner's reasons were as follows: The Commissioner was satisfied that the Council had checked with the most relevant department as to whether any information relating to Part 2 of the request may be held. The Commissioner held that, on the balance of probabilities, the information requested for Part 2 was not held and therefore regulation 12(4)(a) of the EIR was engaged.

### Grounds of appeal

27. The Appellant lodged his appeal on 21 May 2025.

28. The Appellant sought "a positive outcome whereby the information is released".

### The response of the Commissioner

33. The Commissioner lodged his response on 26 June 2025, in which he maintained that the Decision Notice was correct and that the appeal should be dismissed.

34. The Commissioner's response stated that the Appellant had not challenged the Commissioner's decision that, on the balance of probabilities, the Council did not hold the information requested in Part 2 and that there was no evidence that the Council had attempted to mislead the Commissioner.

### Legal Framework

42. A public authority may refuse to disclose information to the extent that it does not hold that information when the applicant's request is received and where, in all of the circumstances of the case, the public interest in maintaining the exception outweighs the public interest in disclosing the information.

43. Whether a public authority holds material is a question of fact to be determined on the balance of probabilities.

45. The relevant test is therefore whether information is, on the evidence, more likely to be held than not held.

46. For practical purposes, if the information is not held then the public interest test is essentially redundant.

### Issues

54. The issues for the Tribunal include whether the Commissioner was correct in holding, on the balance of probabilities, that the Council did not hold any relevant information as sought in Part 2 of the request, at the time that the request was made and that therefore the Council was entitled to rely on regulation 12(4)(a) of the EIR to refuse that part of the request.

### Evidence

55. We read and took account of an open bundle containing 102 pages including indexes.

## Disputed information

Copies of ICNIRP safety certificates related to 5G telecommunications mast installations on the Council's planning portal that had been signed off by "Three UK Limited" (Part 2 of the request). The Council refused on reg 12(4)(a) (not held); the Commissioner upheld this. The Tribunal found the Council did in fact hold at least two such certificates as admitted in the Council's own submissions to the Commissioner.
